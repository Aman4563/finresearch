# Listed-stock research playbook

## Sources, strongest first
1. The company's filings in the store: annual reports (financial statements, notes, MD&A, auditor's report,
   related-party note) and results filings (quarterly consolidated results).
2. NSE data through the tools: price history, shareholding pattern, corporate actions, announcements, results XBRL.
3. Credible secondary sources for peers and industry, cited by URL with the access time.

## What good research on a listed stock checks
- **Consolidated vs standalone:** use consolidated figures unless the question is about the parent; say which.
- **Like-for-like periods:** compare a quarter with the same quarter last year, and TTM with TTM.
- **Earnings quality:** operating cash flow vs PAT over 3–5 years, receivable days, one-offs.
- **Per-share effects:** buybacks and splits change share counts; use EPS and DPS from filings, not recomputed
  from rounded totals.
- **Ownership signals:** promoter holding changes, pledges, bulk deals, employee trusts.
- **Valuation context:** the stock's own range over several years and peers on the same basis (trailing vs forward
  stated explicitly).

## Mistakes to avoid
- Mixing FY and calendar-year figures, or ₹ crore with ₹ million.
- Using a 52-week high/low or a price without its date.
- Treating a dividend's record date as its ex-date.
- Calling a price pattern a forecast.
